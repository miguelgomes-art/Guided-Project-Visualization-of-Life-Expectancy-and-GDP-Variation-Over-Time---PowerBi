# Visualization of Life Expectancy and GDP Variation Over Time - PowerBi



This project was completed on the Dataquest platform as part of one of the PowerBI courses. We will be using visualizations that include scatter charts and bar charts to create reports that will provide insight about our semantic model, including Life Expectancy by Year and GDP Per Capita by Year.

You will practice every phase of a full life-cycle report and dashboard development, including loading data, cleaning data, correcting errors, and choosing and configuring visualizations. This project was quite simple and didn't pose any major challenges for me.

This dashboard presents global socioeconomic trends between 1800 and 2020, revealing a clear correlation between economic development and human well-being.
Until 1950, GDP per capita and life expectancy (stagnant between 30 and 40 years) showed slow growth. Starting in the second half of the 20th century, there was an explosion of wealth led by Europe and, later, by Asia, which also accounts for the largest global population growth (represented by the large orange spheres).

The exponential increase in global wealth after 1950 served as a driver for advances in health and sanitation, raising global life expectancy to the 60–80+ year range by 2020. However, the scatter plot reveals deep historical marks: sharp drops in longevity resulting from wars and pandemics, as well as persistent regional inequality, with Africa still recording health indicators lower than those of other regions.
